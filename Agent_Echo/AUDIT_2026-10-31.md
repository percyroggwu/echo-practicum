# Addendum D - Runs 46-48, the delivery stall and the safety gate

This file continues [AUDIT_2026-10-30.md](AUDIT_2026-10-30.md) without editing it, and its
sections are numbered D.x to follow Addendum C. It audits the two live workflows that
followed it, Gemini 48 and Luna 47, checks what the delivery record says about 46, 47 and 48,
and files the observations that the per-draft evidence supports. The observation rows
continue the numbering of Part B and Addenda A-C at 32.

It is a read-only pass: no live model call, no code change, no test execution, no commit.
Every figure comes from the three artifacts, their exchange captures, the per-draft
checkpoints, the manifests and the current source at the hashes those runs recorded.

The separation rule of Part B still applies: a **system defect** is a case where the message
or the mechanism could not produce the correct action; **model behaviour** is a decision that
was available and not taken; **design** is a mechanism doing what it was built to do with an
outcome nobody has decided. Observations are filed one at a time, and no row averages the two
models.

Observations 32 to 35 are one failure chain and should be read together. The validator rejected
four items without naming any of them (33); the model could not see which reference was wrong,
so it moved two references between items and then repaired one of the four (34); and one of the
four items could never have passed because the only source it cited carries no quotable text
(35). The stall rule then ended the run at the third rejection of the same check (32), although
the submissions differed and the attempt budget would have allowed three more turns.

Observations 36 and 38 are the release side of run 48, and observation 47 is the rating
contract behind both runs. The support review judged 11 findings supported on every assertion
and named two with a failing assertion; the run released none, and the artifact reports the
reason as "supported 0". The mechanism is deliberate (`zero_sugar.py:690`), and this is the
first run in the 43-48 record where it decided a release: runs 44, 45 and 47 record a safety
score of 3 in every rating block, and 43 and 46 completed no review. What it fires on is a
rating whose rationale is the letter `R` and which has no corresponding issue, so a reader
cannot tell whether the score reflects a real concern or an unfilled field.

Observations 40 and 41 are the repair loop's two costs in run 47. The protocol rejections cost
three model turns and changed no judgment: each was caused by one duplicated ID. The content
losses removed the classification paragraph, the pattern, the limits, the percentages note and
both team questions at draft 2, and the three illustrative examples at draft 4; every one of
those blocks had already been in an answer that an earlier round had reviewed. Observation 48
is the presentation defect that generated one of the complaints: the renderer appended an
excerpt that the model had already quoted inside its own sentence, because the appended text
is a substring of the quoted span rather than the whole of it.

Observations 42 and 43 are reviewer readings, not content gaps: the scope and the denominator
are both present in the answer and in the supplied context. Observation 44 is different in
kind: the reviewer withheld two findings for claims the finding does not make, which no check
detects. Observation 45 is an identifier namespace collision between drafts. Observation 46 is
the counting rule already recorded in observation 25, now visible on the limitation list as
well as the pending list.

## D.7 The safety gate and the reviewer readings

Run 48's release decision rests on one rating:

| Rating | Score | Rationale | Matching issue in the same review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Accuracy | 2 | `R` | none |
| Clarity | 2 | `C` | none |
| Completeness | 2 | full sentence about the missing excerpts | the excerpt issue |
| Instruction Following | 2 | `I` | none |
| Relevance | 2 | `R` | none |
| Safety / Appropriateness | 2 | `R` | none |
| Usefulness | 2 | `U` | none |

`zero_sugar.py:690` requires `Safety / Appropriateness == 3` for every finding, and
`original.py:552-554` then withholds the whole set when the score is below 3. A rating whose
rationale is one character and which no issue explains is therefore sufficient to convert 11
supported findings into 0 released. The same one-character pattern appears in every rating
block of both artifacts: in run 48, 3 blocks; in run 47, 9 blocks. Completeness is the only
rating that ever carries a sentence.

Accuracy is read the same way and has a different effect: `zero_sugar.py:1458` forces `revise`
when it is below 3, but it does not withhold a finding. In run 47 accuracy is 2 in six of the
nine blocks while the safety score is 3 in all nine, and the run released five findings. Two
ratings written as a single letter, one blocking acceptance and one blocking release, and
neither is required to say what it observed.

The two reviewers did not apply the same criteria, and the difference is larger than in the
43-45 pair:

- Gemini's completeness review raised one issue, the missing literal excerpts, and did not
  repeat it. Its support review re-raised the same issue as its first of three, with the same
  correction ID prefix, so one obligation was counted by both phases.
- Luna's completeness review raised 3 or 4 issues in every round. The scope complaint appeared
  in 4 of 4 rounds with a new correction ID each time, although `authorized_scope` (M14,
  2026-07-01 to 2026-09-30) and `authorized_teams` were supplied and the instructions say not
  to infer omitted teams from an authorized team name. Round 1 also issued a mandatory
  correction whose own text says the answer provides exactly two questions, which it did.
  `zero_sugar.py:1461-1464` rejects a `revise` verdict that carries no correction, so a
  reviewer that wants to block a compliant answer must attach the block to some obligation;
  the round-1 correction meets that rule by naming an obligation the answer had already met.

Three readings in run 47 are internally inconsistent, and they are worth recording separately
from the scope dispute:

- F11:A2 is assessed `contradicted` with the reason "The phrase 'the two negative comments and
  mixed comments' is internally consistent with the evidence, so the reasoning is sound", while
  the finding-level reason says the premise is supported. The published limitation reproduces
  both halves in one sentence.
- F9:A2 is assessed `insufficient_evidence` and asks that the negative comments be added to a
  proposal's citation basis. The model complied at attempt 3 of the same draft.
- The excerpt requirement moved between rounds: round 1 said no positive excerpt was given,
  round 3 said only the positive one was clearly labelled, round 4 said no literal excerpt
  existed. Only the first and last describe the answer accurately.

F2 and F6 are withheld for assertions that do not appear in the text they review. F2:A2
attributes to F2 the claim that the 13 comments "represent all eligible ... for the full Q3
2026 quarter"; F2's published text says the classification covers the supplied comments. F6:A1
attributes the claim that "Only one team appears ... justifying the absence of suppression";
F6's text asks a question about recognition behaviours. Neither claim is in the block, and no
check compares an assertion to the text it is attached to.

## D.10 Limits of this audit

- Read-only pass over three artifacts, their exchange captures, the per-draft checkpoints, the
  manifests and the current source. No model call, no test run, no code change, no commit.
- Counts are mechanical: calls and events from the captures, findings and release sets from the
  artifacts, characters and token counts from the logged requests, duplication from Jaccard
  overlap with the method stated in D.5.
- No label, citation, correction or claim was graded semantically. Where the answer and the
  review disagree, this file reports both and does not decide which reading of the question is
  right.
- One pair per provider, with generator and reviewer served by the same model in each run. Any
  difference between 47 and 48 can be a provider difference, a reviewer difference, or both.
- Run 48 has one reviewed draft. Its draft table describes one answer and three rejected
  repair submissions, not four reviewed answers, and no completeness or support decision was
  taken on any of the three.
- The `excerpt_source` item count in D.5 is read from the rejected submissions as captured. The
  validator does not report it, so the count is the auditor's, not the system's.
- The 46 network artifact is included for the record only; its capture was read for the
  terminal events, not event by event, so its figures are the artifact's counters.
- Nothing here relaxes the correction-state rule, the evidence-ID rule, the draft cap or the
  attempt cap, and nothing reduces findings, sources or assertions to make an outcome fit.
