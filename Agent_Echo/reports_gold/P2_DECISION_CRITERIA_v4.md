## Rules

1. **No stop on protocol failure.** No prompt line is under decision, so a protocol failure does
   not stop the measurement. It is diagnosed and counted as a failed case-run, so the denominator
   stays at 28 case-runs per profile (not the 26 and 23 completed in the first run).
2. **`plan()` fix.** After `c23bf23` the recheck always adds a turn, and a turn is added only
   after a rejection, so no case-run that passed before can fail. This is a structural check of
   the implementation, not an uncertain test of the model:
   - Pass: every rejection with `validation_code: clarification_recheck` has
     `feedback_retry_available: true`, and every such rejection is followed by another model
     turn. In the first run, 5 of the 17 rechecks had that flag `false`, all of them at attempt 2.
   - The check fails when the fix is not in effect, or when the elapsed-time (300 s) or
     model-call (5) budget ended the case-run first. Diagnose before deciding.
   - The fix cannot lower the pass count. The extra turn exists only after a rejection, and under
     the old code those case-runs had no turn left and ended in a protocol failure.
3. **No 85% threshold in this run.** The overall pass count is reported against the baseline,
   without a pass or fail line, over 28 case-runs per profile (rule 1). It becomes the reference
   for later planning work. Two different conditions apply later, and they are kept apart:
   - Evidence to open a rule (the v1 condition): the same failure appears in both repeats or in
     two different cases, and the commit cites it.
   - Verification to keep a rule, measured on the new code: its target failure passes in both
     repeats on both profiles, and the overall pass count does not fall below this re-run's count
     on either profile.
4. **Hard cases unchanged.** P1 (the runs 63-66 question), P10 and P11 stay hard. All three
   failed in the first run, and P1 failed again in live run 69. They are reported separately,
   and no decision in this run depends on them.
5. **Reported per case-run:**
   - the plan mismatches;
   - the model calls;
   - the raw plan, as a diagnostic;
   - the recheck: its attempt, `feedback_retry_available`, and the number of model turns.
     `planning_diagnostics` alone is not enough, because an accepted attempt does not always
     leave an entry (apinex_test P12 r2 has one entry, the recheck, and completes); turns are
     counted from `model_calls` and `raw_calls`.

## False clarifications (new observable)

A **false clarification** is a case-run where the plan asks the user for something
(`clarify: true`) on a case whose expected plan does not ask (`clarify: false`): P1, P2-P11 and
P14. It is reported per case-run with the case, the repeat, the attempt that proposed it, whether
the recheck fired, and the final outcome.

- It counts as a failed case-run: `gold_plans.mismatches()` compares only `clarify` on such a
  case, so the pass count already includes it. A false clarification does not decide by itself,
  but it is not left out of the count either.
- The new observable is the recheck's extra turn. In the first run the five Gemini failures (P8
  r1, P8 r2, P9 r1, P12 r1, P13 r1) had no turn left after the recheck; under the fix they reach
  a third attempt. On P8 and P9 (no clarification expected), a third attempt that still asks is
  a `clarify` mismatch: the defect is fixed and the pass count does not rise. On P12 and P13
  (clarification expected), a third attempt that still asks passes. Both facts are reported.
